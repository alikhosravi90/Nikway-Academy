## Evidence method and limits

Only issuer/project/publisher sources support observations. The tool returned official-domain indexed pages and direct page opens; it did not archive copyrighted full text. Exact URLs and heading locators are recorded below and in JSON. `verified` is deliberately narrow: the stated page facts were observed, not every claim in a book, an item's latest immutable edition, legal reuse authorization, scientific effectiveness, or NIKWAY conformance. Where direct retrieval failed (ICICLE 403; LEBOK no readable lines), the register explicitly records indexed evidence limitations. Search-engine crawl/publication estimates were not treated as publication dates.

Unknown publication dates stay UNKNOWN. Online pages are mutable and no snapshot hashes are available. A reviewer should pin normative editions and verify source revisions before downstream approval. No source content was translated, no protected standard clauses or diagrams were reproduced, and no blanket open license was inferred. All `allowedUses` arrays are empty because protected-material reuse has not been cleared. This does not describe original NIKWAY commentary as copied source material.

## PDCA versus PDSA: source-grounded teaching boundary

[ASQ, The Plan-do-check-act Procedure](https://asq.org/quality-resources/pdca-cycle) provides the PDCA reference: plan a change, test it at small scale, inspect results and learning, and decide subsequent action. [The Deming Institute, PDSA and PDCA](https://deming.org/explore/pdsa/) explicitly distinguishes PDSA, with emphasis on predictions, theory and studying actual outcomes to revise understanding. ASQ's page groups historical variant names; the Institute makes a substantive distinction. Preserve both attributed perspectives rather than presenting the terms as universal synonyms or asserting that Deming originated exactly the PDCA formulation taught here.

## Proposed PDCA pilot content and evaluation (not source quotations)

**Competency:** Given a bounded process problem and a synthetic dataset, the learner can document a complete PDCA cycle, distinguish observations from explanations, and justify a next action using the test evidence.

**Outcomes:** Identify a measurable problem and baseline; specify a small test and comparison criteria; calculate and interpret supplied results with limitations; choose and justify adopt/adapt/abandon or further testing; explain the PDCA/PDSA distinction with source attribution. These are original draft outcomes, not validated competency standards.

**Applied task:** Use synthetic content-review turnaround records. Plan one process change, state a prediction and measurement rule, run a simulated small test, check results and missing data, and document the next iteration. Prediction is a useful NIKWAY design choice informed by the PDSA distinction; it must not be misrepresented as a verbatim ASQ requirement.

**Rubric proposal:** Score problem/baseline clarity, test design, evidence interpretation, justified next action, and terminology/provenance separately. Thresholds, weights, reviewer agreement procedure and real-learner success criteria remain pending verification and owner review. A good synthetic example proves only that the example can be scored; it proves no real learning gain.

**Evaluation evidence:** Before a real pilot, specify population, consent/access, pre/post comparable tasks, scoring version, missing-data handling, observation window and success threshold. Record raw counts and uncertainty. Separate process metrics (citation completeness, rights clearance, import correctness) from learner performance. A pre/post difference alone does not establish causality; alternative explanations and design limits must remain visible.
